## 3. Operational Requirements

The US-D1 Pro will perform optimally if the operational requirements below are fully satisfied. Failure to meet the operational requirements may cause a decrease in performance, accuracy, or reliability of the US-D1 Pro altimeter and is not advised.

## 3.1 Obstruction to the US-D1 Pro Field of View

Objects or aircraft structures that are located within the US-D1 Pro’s conical field of view, which obstruct the radar’s view of the ground, may cause multipath reflections or other degradative phenomena to occur. The US-D1 Pro should be mounted a safe distance away from the landing gear and any other components of the aircraft below it.

## 3.2 Excessive Pitch/Roll

Pitch and roll angles that exceed 20° and 20°, respectively, may also cause the performance of the US-D1 Pro to worsen. This sensitivity increases with altitude.

## 3.3 Terrain

Terrain with poor reflectivity may cause the performance of the US-D1 Pro to worsen. Flying at the limit of the US-D1 Pro’s range over dry, loose soil, such as tilled farmland or sand, is not recommended.

## 3.4 Power Source

Any power source used to operate the US-D1 Pro that does not provide the proper power and voltage can worsen the performance of the device or cause its operation to stop altogether. Moveover if the power voltage is significantly more than the input voltage upper limit, it may permanently damage the device.

## 3.5 Orientation of US-D1 Pro

The US-D1 Pro must be mounted upon the aircraft in such a manner as to be fully horizontal, its radome directly facing the ground. Any angle of inclination may degrade performance.

## 3.6 Minimum and Maximum Operating Altitude

Operating the US-D1 Pro at altitudes below its minimum altitude and above 120 meters will result in a degradation of performance and potentially erroneous measurements.
